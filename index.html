The problem statement, explained

Business goal: management wants +20% sales. That is a marketing target, but a sales target is only real if the company can actually deliver what customers order.

The unknown: nobody knows whether EmployeesInventoryProduction capacityLogistics can absorb the extra demand. If they can’t, the company gets stock-outs, late deliveries, burnt-out staff, lost customers and a damaged brand: the campaign succeeds while the business fails.

What you must build: a what-if simulator, an AI scenario-analysis system. Decision-makers change a strategy (more marketing, price change, hiring, a third shift, outsourcing, more vehicles, safety stock) and instantly see the consequences on demand, fulfilment, cost, profit and risk.

Your role as data analyst

  1. Frame demand: baseline × growth × price elasticity × seasonality.
  2. Model each constraint (people, machines, raw material, stock, fleet) as a monthly capacity.
  3. Find the bottleneck: the lowest capacity decides what can really be sold.
  4. Simulate scenarios + uncertainty (Monte Carlo) and cost each one.
  5. Let an AI agent explain results, recommend the cheapest feasible plan, and answer “what if…” questions.

Key ideas shown in this dashboard

  • Theory of constraints: sellable units = min(demand, workforce, machines, supply, logistics) + stock buffer.
  • Seasonality: Oct–Dec peak (festival season) is where plans break.
  • Trade-offs: overtime is cheap but raises burnout risk; outsourcing is quick but costs margin; hiring needs ramp-up time.
